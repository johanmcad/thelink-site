How it works

Quiet by design.

Link is a bridge. On one side, your home network. On the other, the control port your preamplifier already has. In between, a small amount of software whose first job is to never do anything surprising.

The bridge

Link translates taps into the preamplifier’s own control language and listens to everything it says back. Nothing is interpreted, nothing is invented.

The mirror

Link shows what the preamplifier has confirmed. Never what it assumes. Turn the knob on the front panel and every screen follows, because they are all looking at the same thing.

Relative only

Link never sends an absolute volume. One step up, one step down, each one acknowledged by the preamplifier before the next. There is no gesture that can jump the volume — no drag, no tap on the dial.

The soft limit

A ceiling you set, enforced inside the box. Volume-up commands stop at the limit no matter which phone sends them, and lowering the limit below the current volume walks the volume down to meet it.

The rate limit

At most one step every 150 milliseconds, whatever any client sends. Holding the button is the phone repeating single steps; let go, or lose the connection, and the stepping stops at once.

The unlock

Going above the limit is a deliberate act — a separate control, shown only once the volume has reached the ceiling — and it undoes itself after thirty seconds.

Nothing leaves your home

The remote runs on your own network. There is no account, no cloud service and no tracking. The only thing Link ever fetches from the internet is its own signed software update.

Mute

One tap, no confirmation, always. Some things should not have friction.

Designed on a system driven by 2 × 450 W of amplification. The safety model is not an afterthought.
